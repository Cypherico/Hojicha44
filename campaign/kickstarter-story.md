# Hojicha44

**Kickstarter subtitle**  
A low-profile split keyboard with 44 keys, two dials, and a design you can make your own.

## A little room between the halves

Room for a cup of tea, a notebook, or simply some space between your hands. Hojicha44 is a small split keyboard with staggered columns, four keys for each thumb, and a dial on either side. The halves can sit close together one day and a little farther apart the next.

## The sound of a key

A keystroke begins at the switch and travels through the plate to the case. I wanted to pay attention to that whole journey.

Inside Hojicha44, the polycarbonate switch plate sits on PORON gaskets. Layers of foam give us room to tune the sound, and the low-profile Choc v1 switches can be swapped without soldering. A change as small as a different switch can give the same board a different voice.

## Forty-four keys, two dials

The letters are where you’d expect them on QWERTY. Numbers, symbols, and navigation live on layers within thumb’s reach. There’s a little to learn, so the build guide will include a keymap to keep beside the board while the layout becomes familiar.

The dials can take on everyday jobs like scrolling and volume. The halves talk to each other over Bluetooth, and the left half can connect to your computer by USB-C. The keyboard can also connect to the computer over Bluetooth.

## The design is yours to build on

There’s a pleasure in being able to change a thing you use every day. A new job for a dial. A case printed in a favourite colour. A different arrangement of keys for the work you do.

The PCB sources, case generator, plate drawings, and ZMK configuration are available to explore. I want the build guide to make it easier to find where each of those changes begins. A keymap shared by one person might be just what someone else was looking for.

## What backing makes possible

I’m raising funds to put the first matched kits into people’s hands. A pledge helps pay for a pilot run, fit and assembly checks, and a first batch of parts. A kit brings the PCB pair, plates, and cases together so builders can start with parts made to fit each other. I’ll share what we learn from the pilot in the public build guide, where it can help people sourcing and building their own boards too.

The PCB, plate, and case drawings are ready for a first physical build. Before opening pledges, I’ll show the working prototype, share a typing recording, and list exactly what comes in the kit. After funding, the pilot run will give us a chance to check that the parts fit and assemble consistently before making the backer batch.
