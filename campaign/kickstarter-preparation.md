# Preparing Hojicha44 for Kickstarter

## Page format

Draft in Markdown for revisions, then paste the story into Kickstarter’s editor as native headings, paragraphs and lists. Insert individual photographs and diagrams at the relevant points. Keep prices, shipping, delivery dates and kit contents in Kickstarter’s reward fields as well as any concise comparison in the story. A single tall JPEG makes the copy hard to read on mobile and hard to revise. A PDF can be a separate build guide. [Story editor](https://updates.kickstarter.com/text-editor-2-0/) · [Campaign page guide](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-lessons/guide-to-kickstarter-campaign-page/)

| Asset | Prepare |
| --- | --- |
| Project thumbnail | A photo of the working pair, JPEG, 16:9. Kickstarter recommends 1024 × 576 px and a 50 MB maximum. Keep both halves clear of crop edges. |
| Story images | Individual JPEG photos and PNG diagrams. Keep important copy in the editor and check legibility in the mobile preview. |
| Project video | A 60–90 second working-prototype demo. Prepare a captioned MP4 master and check the current uploader before exporting the final file. |
| Typing demo | A separate clip with natural sound and the build details in the caption. People should be able to hear the switches before the pitch starts. |
| Rewards and FAQ | Native reward cards, with included parts, assembly needs and delivery estimates. Use the FAQ for layout, connectivity, compatibility and licensing. |

[Kickstarter image guidance](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/images) · [Story handbook](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/handbook/your_story?lang=en)

## Prototype footage

Kickstarter’s hardware guidance requires a working prototype and prohibits photorealistic concept imagery that could be mistaken for a finished product. Use the Blender shots to plan angles and lighting. Photograph and film the real board for the thumbnail, hero and feature demonstrations. Use clearly identified technical diagrams to explain internal construction. [Current rules](https://www.kickstarter.com/rules) · [Hardware launch guidance](https://updates.kickstarter.com/how-to-launch-a-product-on-kickstarter-part-1-creating-your-prototype-and-manufacturing-plan/) · [Prototype imagery guidance](https://www.kickstarter.com/blog/pointers-for-sharing-your-prototype)

| In order | Capture |
| --- | --- |
| Open with the keyboard | A few seconds of real typing, then move both halves into position. |
| Show that it is yours to change | Pull and replace a switch, change a dial mapping, then show a case or plate source file alongside the physical assembly. |
| Show sound and feel | Record both halves with fixed microphone position and gain. Use the same switches, caps and desk if comparing foam configurations. Photograph the gaskets and plate during teardown. |
| Show everyday use | Type on the base layer, use a thumb layer, and turn and press both dials. Show Bluetooth and USB-C host connections in the working build. |
| Explain the pledge | Show the physical prototype and parts the proposed kit would contain. State the exact first-batch work that funding makes possible. |

## What people back

Lead with one physical reward, a **matched kit**, and offer a pledge without a reward for people building from source. Cost the kit against one confirmed configuration. Candidate contents include the PCB pair, cases, plates, gasket pads, foam and mechanical hardware. Confirm controller, battery, knob, switch and keycap inclusion before setting any price. Show the actual kit spread with the omissions listed next to it. An assembled option makes sense only if assembly, testing, warranty handling and delivery can be priced reliably.

Backers fund pilot units, supplier minimums, incoming inspection, packing, spares and the first batch. The wider community gets the editable sources, tested build guide and documented changes. Start with one configuration, one bill of materials and one realistic production plan. Build the funding goal from supplier quotes, per-unit fulfillment, fixed testing and pilot costs, payment and platform fees, taxes where applicable, and a contingency. Leave prices and delivery dates open until the numbers support them. [Kickstarter manufacturing and budgeting guidance](https://updates.kickstarter.com/how-to-launch-a-product-on-kickstarter-part-1-creating-your-prototype-and-manufacturing-plan/)

## Write from evidence

- **Open design.** The repository has editable files. A project-wide licence was not found in this checkout. Confirm licences for hardware, firmware and documentation and check third-party terms before making a broad legal promise about reuse, redistribution or commercial builds.
- **Sound and ergonomics.** Describe the gasket and foam construction now. Use matched physical recordings and a real thumb-reach demo to support how the board feels and sounds. Avoid promising a specific sound or health outcome.
- **Connectivity.** USB-C connects the left half to the host. The halves use Bluetooth. Treat the TRRS jack as a development detail unless a supported consumer use is confirmed.
- **First article.** The v0.5.4 fabrication files are a candidate. Build and check a pair, record the results and photograph the exact revision demonstrated. Verify the proposed kit, battery shipping plan if applicable, and fulfillment estimates before submission. [Repository first-article checks](../spec/first-article.md)

Suggested page order is hero and typing demo, open design, gasket construction, layout and connectivity, reward contents, use of funds, then prototype status and delivery risks. Preview on mobile and ask a first-time split user to identify what the kit includes and how the two halves connect.
